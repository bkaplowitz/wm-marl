# Seed instability: matched-state diagnosis, 6 September 2026

The strongest measured lead is compounding error in the self-fed observation
interface, especially on trajectories collected by stronger policies, expressed
as premature imagined deaths and incorrect action support.
The weak final models make substantially more of these errors than the strong
model on the same real states. Supplying factual observation embeddings during
the simulated rollout removes most of the weak models' excess error. Supplying
factual liveness alone helps less. This identifies a simulator failure and its
feedback pathway; an improvement in real win rate from repairing it remains to
be demonstrated.

## What was actually measured

- Extracted numeric learning timelines from 42 matrix attempts and 10 started
  sweep attempts at 15:26:46 UTC. Failed and incomplete attempts remain explicit.
- Collected 160 new real episodes: 32 per frozen matrix checkpoint for
  `3s_vs_3z` seeds 0/1/2 and `3s_vs_5z` seeds 0/2. These sample the learned
  policy, without collection exploration mixing. They are value diagnostics,
  distinct from the original 100-episode greedy final evaluations.
- Verified unchanged model-state and counter hashes before/after each calibration.
- Scored all three `3s_vs_3z` models on identical 96 recorded roots from 55
  contributing episodes, mixing all three policies. Scored both `3s_vs_5z`
  models on another identical 96 roots from 45 contributing episodes.
- Each model re-encodes raw observations into its own representation. Recorded
  actions and stochastic draw keys are shared across diagnostic rollout paths.
  No cross-model latent substitution or alternative-action ground truth is used.
- Compared factual-history, self-fed, observation-only oracle, liveness-only
  oracle, and combined oracle paths. Oracle feedback changes the next simulated
  state; predicted output heads are still scored against the recorded outcome.

## 1. A measurable cross-seed difference in imagined survival

False-death rate is the fraction of root-live agents still alive in the actual
recorded state at H5 that the self-fed rollout declares dead.

| Map | Training seed | Original greedy final100 wins | H5 false deaths | H5 false-death rate | Liveness Brier |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3s_vs_3z | 0 | 15% | 21 / 201 | 10.45% | .0926 |
| 3s_vs_3z | 1 | 75% | 4 / 201 | 1.99% | .0240 |
| 3s_vs_3z | 2 | 9% | 13 / 201 | 6.47% | .0714 |
| 3s_vs_5z | 0 | 4% | 15 / 180 | 8.33% | .0827 |
| 3s_vs_5z | 2 | 64% | 5 / 180 | 2.78% | .0365 |

On `3s_vs_3z`, H1 false-death rates are only 1.44%, 1.91%, and 2.39%.
By H8 they are 18.46%, 2.56%, and 8.72%. The gap develops during feedback.
H5 is the actual training imagination horizon; H8 is an additional stress test.

Paired bootstrap resampling of whole contributing episodes (4,000 shared draws)
gives H5 false-death differences relative to strong seed 1 of +8.46 percentage
points [4.39, 13.33] for seed 0 and +4.48 [1.69, 8.11] for seed 2.
On `3s_vs_5z`, the difference is +5.56 points [0.00, 12.20]; that replication is
less conclusive. These intervals describe diagnostic-sample uncertainty, not
statistical significance across training seeds or a causal win-rate effect.

Actual H5 deaths are scarce: eight in the first bank and two in the second.
The evidence is stronger for invented premature deaths than missed actual deaths.

### Where the error occurs matters

Stratifying the same roots by the policy that collected them changes the
interpretation: the error is concentrated on the stronger policy's trajectories.
On the 58 actually-alive H5 agent positions from `3s_vs_3z` strong seed 1, the
three models invent 12, 0, and 8 deaths. Weak seed 0 invents only 2/71 deaths on
its own collected roots. On `3s_vs_5z`, weak seed 0 invents 0/98 deaths on its own
roots but 15/82 on strong seed 2's roots; the strong model invents 1/82 there.

This supports a narrower claim than uniformly poor simulation: weak models have
a blind spot for trajectories generated by stronger policies. Different replay
coverage and extrapolation may contribute. We have not shown whether this blind
spot preceded or followed the policy's failure to discover those behaviors.
The mixed-policy bank is therefore deliberately a coverage stress test, not an
estimate of the weak policy's own on-policy error. A plausible self-reinforcing
learning loop is poor coverage, bad simulation of better behaviors, and difficulty
learning those behaviors; its temporal direction still needs checkpoint forks.

## 2. Observation feedback is the larger contributor

| Map / seed | Self-fed H5 false deaths | Actual observation feedback only | Actual liveness feedback only | Both |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3s_vs_3z / 0 | 21 | 5 | 20 | 4 |
| 3s_vs_3z / 1 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 3s_vs_3z / 2 | 13 | 6 | 13 | 5 |
| 3s_vs_5z / 0 | 15 | 2 | 10 | 0 |
| 3s_vs_5z / 2 | 5 | 3 | 0 | 0 |

For weak `3s_vs_3z` seed 0, observation-only feedback reduces liveness Brier
from .0926 to .0234 and action-mask false negatives from .1323 to .0474.
The same intervention reduces seed 2 Brier from .0714 to .0315.
On weak `3s_vs_5z` seed 0, Brier falls from .0827 to .0159.

The deployed rollout completes local history from a predicted observation
embedding, then feeds that local state into the next joint prediction.
It also uses `next_alive = current_alive & present & (alive_probability >= .5)`.
An erroneous death is absorbing: subsequent imagined action support becomes
no-op, future active-agent policy decisions are removed, and critic context uses
the dead-agent state. This amplifies interface errors. It does not mean factual
team returns are deliberately cut whenever an individual dies.

Relevant implementation: `src/majepa/marl/core.py`, the hard-liveness CTDE
imagination transition around lines 2816–2848. The existing recurrent objective
in `src/majepa/training/self_fed.py` already retains supervision after a predicted
death; simply unmasking those targets is not a missing fix.

## 3. Blanket critic overoptimism is not a good final-checkpoint explanation

Actual discounted-return calibration under the sampled learned policy:

| Map / seed | Mean initial predicted value | Mean actual discounted return | Alive-state value RMSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3s_vs_3z / 0 | 12.815 | 12.665 | 2.460 |
| 3s_vs_3z / 1 | 15.190 | 15.015 | 2.721 |
| 3s_vs_3z / 2 | 12.025 | 11.693 | 2.084 |
| 3s_vs_5z / 0 | 15.458 | 14.542 | 2.959 |
| 3s_vs_5z / 2 | 16.398 | 15.631 | 3.047 |

Real-return explained variance is approximately .66–.73 on `3s_vs_3z`, rather
than the near-.99 numbers against constructed training targets. But poor seeds
do not have clearly worse factual calibration. Five-step reward-sum prediction
error likewise does not reliably separate strong and weak seeds on the common
roots. Early value errors and wrong action rankings remain possible; final
state-value calibration cannot rule them out.

The factual-value and representation arms remain useful controls. Their first
completed `2s3z` seed is mixed: base 25%, fact 13%, factrep 14%, actor1 16%,
fact-a1 26%, frep-a1 36%, entlow 15%. These are seven individual training seeds,
not evidence yet that an arm improves cross-seed stability.

## 4. Reward-rich nonwinning behavior is a separate observable problem

Original `3s_vs_5z` finals have wins 4%, 0%, 64% and timeouts 85%, 96%, 32%.
Their mean shaped returns are much closer: 19.85, 19.36, 20.98. The weak seeds
leave more enemies alive and accrue substantially more enemy shield regeneration.
Fresh sampled-policy episodes reproduce this pattern. A value function can fit
the shaped return reasonably well while the policy wins very little.

This does not establish deliberate reward exploitation or justify changing the
benchmark reward. DMAWM also receives SMAC's shaped reward. Rescoring the same
new trajectories with DMAWM's .99 discount did not demonstrate an advantage
over our `1 - 1/333`; copying the discount is not an evidence-backed fix here.

## What DMAWM tells us

At pinned commit `2acaaeb82805b55d275e3ce08ac8f713ec9afbb2`, DMAWM's world
model trains on factual successor target-critic values with bounded cumulative
joint importance weights (cap 4). Value gradients reach world-model features,
followed by a separate critic update. This supports our earlier two hypotheses
as legitimate mechanisms, without establishing that they explain this instability.
Our clipped V-trace and local auxiliary value loss are not exact replicas.
[DMAWM value training](https://github.com/DiXue98/DMAWM/blob/2acaaeb82805b55d275e3ce08ac8f713ec9afbb2/world_model.py#L439-L533).

DMAWM imagines RSSM prior states and predicts action availability and continuation.
Its released imagination loop does not add our hard absorbing per-agent alive
gate or recursively complete local history from a separate predicted observation
embedding. That makes our interface an architecture-specific responsibility;
we cannot assume DMAWM's PPO hyperparameters solve it.
[DMAWM imagination](https://github.com/DiXue98/DMAWM/blob/2acaaeb82805b55d275e3ce08ac8f713ec9afbb2/world_model.py#L179-L263).

## Next causal comparison

Prioritize simulator feedback consistency, while keeping factual-value and actor
update controls already running. A discriminating comparison is:

1. Fork a retained intermediate reference checkpoint, with the same raw replay,
   into unchanged BPTT2 and a repair that explicitly aligns the local posterior
   induced by predicted observations with the factual-history posterior across
   the self-fed rollout. Keep the factual target and local consumer parameters
   frozen for this auxiliary producer gradient. This requires implementing the
   objective safely with BPTT2; enabling the current consumer-KL flag alone is
   rejected by the existing configuration contract.
2. Include increased recurrent-root coverage as a separate control: the present
   objective samples eight roots per update. This distinguishes insufficient
   exposure within available replay from a missing interface objective. Sampling
   more roots cannot create successful trajectories absent from replay. A frozen
   offline comparison on equal added strong-policy data can separately test that
   coverage hypothesis; it is a diagnostic, not a matched-budget benchmark run.
   Do not combine coverage, EMA,
   actor capacity, and policy update changes in the first causal comparison.
3. Require lower H5 false-death and action-support error on held-out common roots
   without increasing missed deaths, then measure actual policy improvement and
   repeat across seeds. Passing the simulator metric alone is insufficient.
4. Measure full executable policy KL before/after world-model updates on fixed raw
   histories. PPO's logged actor KL on frozen features misses policy changes from
   encoder/history updates. This is an unresolved mechanism, not a measured cause.

These are proposed next training comparisons, not jobs silently added to the
current sweep. The initial common-root audit does not establish alternative-action
ranking accuracy, cover `2s3z`/`3s_vs_4z`, or explain MMM's seed differences.
