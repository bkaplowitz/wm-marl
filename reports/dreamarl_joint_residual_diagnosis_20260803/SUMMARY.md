# Joint-Residual Transition Diagnosis

## Decision

Reject the current zero-gated joint-residual transition. It did not produce a
more generally useful multi-agent world model. It fit the narrow replay
distribution generated by its own collapsed policy, but its recursive visual
predictions degraded sharply on trajectories from the stronger local-memory
policy.

This rejects the implementation

\[
F_{\mathrm{local}}(z_t^i, a_t^i) +
\alpha\,\Delta F_{\mathrm{joint}}(z_t^{1:A}, a_t^{1:A})
\]

as currently integrated. It does **not** reject joint context or synchronous
multi-agent imagination as research directions.

## Control Result

At the 50K Externality gate, the joint-residual policy had a final
deterministic evaluation mean of approximately `0.87`, compared with `21.59`
for the matched local-memory reference. Its last training episodes remained
near zero while the reference repeatedly produced substantially rewarding
episodes.

The control result alone established that the branch should not be promoted.
The diagnostics below determine whether the failure was primarily in control
or in the learned simulator.

## Protocol

- Environment: Externality Mushrooms Dense.
- Checkpoints: completed seed-0 local-memory and joint-residual 50K runs.
- Datasets: 20 complete replay windows of 64 steps from each policy.
- State inference: first 32 logged steps.
- Evaluation: recursive open-loop prediction over the remaining 32 steps using
  logged actions.
- Horizons: 1, 2, 4, and 8.
- Comparisons: both frozen checkpoints were evaluated on the exact same replay
  windows in each direction.
- Primary metric: decoded image MAE. This is observable and comparable across
  independently trained latent coordinate systems.
- Uncertainty: paired trajectory bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, 20,000
  resamples.

## Strong-Policy Replay

These windows came from the successful local-memory policy. Lower image MAE is
better.

| Horizon | Local memory | Joint residual | Joint / local | Paired difference (95% CI) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.0155 | 0.0356 | 2.29x | +0.0200 [+0.0138, +0.0269] |
| 2 | 0.0154 | 0.0429 | 2.78x | +0.0275 [+0.0219, +0.0332] |
| 4 | 0.0159 | 0.0575 | 3.61x | +0.0416 [+0.0338, +0.0496] |
| 8 | 0.0159 | 0.0691 | 4.35x | +0.0532 [+0.0448, +0.0619] |

The local model remains nearly flat through horizon 8. The joint model starts
worse and accumulates substantially more recursive error at every horizon.
This is incompatible with the hypothesis that the joint residual learned a
better simulator but was merely used badly by the actor-critic.

Reward prediction is sparse and therefore secondary. The joint model was
worse at h1 (`0.1801` versus `0.0599`) and h8 (`0.1089` versus `0.0589`), while
the apparent h2/h4 gains had confidence intervals that reached or crossed zero.

## Joint-Policy Replay

On replay generated by its own failed policy, the joint model appears stronger
at short horizons:

| Horizon | Local memory | Joint residual | Joint / local | Paired difference (95% CI) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.0221 | 0.0091 | 0.41x | -0.0130 [-0.0161, -0.0101] |
| 2 | 0.0289 | 0.0119 | 0.41x | -0.0171 [-0.0239, -0.0113] |
| 4 | 0.0339 | 0.0269 | 0.79x | -0.0070 [-0.0161, +0.0027] |
| 8 | 0.0395 | 0.0549 | 1.39x | +0.0154 [+0.0021, +0.0293] |

Even on this favorable distribution, the advantage reverses by h8. More
importantly, the two replay distributions are not equally difficult:

| Replay source | Mean team return per 64 steps | Rewarding steps | Mean absolute team reward per step |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local-memory policy | 20.35 | 15.8% | 0.334 |
| Joint-residual policy | 5.00 | 4.4% | 0.084 |

The joint checkpoint therefore learned its own low-return visitation
distribution well, but did not generalize to the richer behavior of the policy
we want to preserve. This is policy/model co-adaptation, not robust dynamics
improvement.

## Interaction Branch

The residual branch was active rather than stuck at its zero initialization.
On strong-policy replay its mean RMS magnitude was about `0.23` for the
deterministic state and `1.15` at h1 for memory (`0.89` at h8). However, its
per-trajectory correlation with latent and reward error was unstable: standard
deviations were typically `0.4` to `0.6`, and mean correlations stayed small or
changed sign across horizons.

The branch was therefore making material recurrent changes without a reliable
relationship to where prediction needed correction.

## Interpretation

The measured failure is in the transition design itself:

1. The joint residual is added recursively to the local transition, so an
   uncalibrated correction is repeatedly reintroduced during imagination.
2. The memory posterior target remains locally inferred from observations,
   while the prior receives joint context. That creates an asymmetric target
   contract for the largest residual component.
3. The learned correction specializes to the collapsed policy distribution and
   degrades under the stronger policy's state-action distribution.

These findings are sufficient to stop this branch. Actor/critic adaptation,
longer training, or tuning the residual coefficient would confound the result
instead of addressing it.

## Next Architecture Gate

Preserve the validated local-memory state and decentralized actor. The next
joint-context experiment should create a separate world-simulator context
before transition prediction, rather than add an unconstrained correction to
the persistent local state:

\[
c_t^{1:A}=\operatorname{AgentAttention}(\operatorname{sg}(z_t^{1:A}),a_t^{1:A})
\]

\[
\hat z_{t+1}^{i}=F(z_t^i,a_t^i,c_t^i).
\]

The actor continues to consume only `z_t^i`. Promotion requires all three:

1. zero-context numerical equivalence with the local-memory reference;
2. improved matched recursive prediction on strong-policy replay, especially
   h4/h8 decoded image error;
3. improved Externality control without regression on Pure Coordination.

No broader benchmark run is justified until this gate passes.
